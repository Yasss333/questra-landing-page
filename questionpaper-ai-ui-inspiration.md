# QuestionPaper.ai UI/UX Inspiration for Questra

**Reviewed:** 2 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Capture public-site patterns that may inform Questra’s customer-facing marketing site. This is design research, not an instruction to copy the competitor’s brand, copy, claims, or product behavior.

## Pages reviewed

- [Homepage](https://questionpaper.ai/) — hero, navigation, product previews, benefits, workflow, pricing, proof, testimonials, and closing CTA.
- [Features](https://questionpaper.ai/features) — feature explanations and benefit cards.
- [Pricing](https://questionpaper.ai/pricing) — individual credit packs, institutional plan, feature list, and pricing FAQ.
- [FAQs](https://questionpaper.ai/faq) — split introduction and accordion layout.
- [Contact](https://questionpaper.ai/contact-us) — contact details beside a form.
- [About](https://questionpaper.ai/about-us) — company positioning and supporting feature claims.

This review covers the public marketing surface only. No sign-in or private product screens were inspected.

## What the site does visually

QuestionPaper.ai uses a white canvas with pale blush section backgrounds, bright magenta accents, and dark slate text. Large soft curves and pink shapes separate or decorate sections. Its homepage hero is asymmetrical: headline, short explanation, and one strong CTA on the left; angled product screenshots over a large pink shape on the right. Feature and FAQ pages begin with centered section titles, then move into card or split-column content. A vertical “Sign Up Now” tab stays at the right edge as a persistent conversion prompt.

The visual style is energetic and consumer-oriented. Questra already has an approved blue, green, and navy identity; use the layout and interaction ideas below while keeping Questra’s own palette and more institutional tone.

## UX observations and cautions

- **Clear primary route:** The main navigation exposes About, Pricing, Features, FAQs, Contact, and Sign In. Questra can keep its richer Platform and Resources menus but should make the demo action the clearest institutional next step.
- **Repeated conversion prompts:** The site offers access/signup actions in the hero, a fixed edge tab, pricing cards, and a closing CTA. For Questra, repeat “Book a 30-minute demo” at meaningful points without letting a sticky control compete with navigation or content.
- **Strong visual proof, but watch density:** The hero’s layered product collage quickly signals that a working application exists. Ensure any Questra visual remains legible at laptop and phone widths and is labelled as illustrative when it uses sample data.
- **Animation needs restraint:** The rotating/typewriter headline changes while reading. Questra should favor stable, readable copy and respect reduced-motion preferences; keep the existing moving grid subtle and decorative.
- **Long feature inventory:** The Features page places many capabilities into a long sequence of cards. Questra should preserve its grouped sections and use dedicated pages for detailed capabilities rather than expanding the homepage indefinitely.
- **Institutional and individual journeys differ:** QuestionPaper.ai mixes self-serve credit purchases and institutional enquiries. Questra’s marketing page should prioritize institutions, academic teams, and demos unless a self-serve buying path is explicitly approved.

## Recommended Questra direction

1. Keep the Questra logo, approved palette, current headline, and moving grid.
2. Use one polished product view early in the page to make the SaaS experience tangible.
3. Organize capability cards around academic outcomes and roles, then connect them to the detailed feature pages already in the site.
4. Keep one consistent demo CTA in the navbar, after key proof sections, and in the closing section.
5. Add social proof or pricing only after the institution has approved the exact assets and claims.

## Source and limits

The observations above come from the public pages linked in “Pages reviewed” and browser-rendered views observed on 2 November 2026. The site’s prices, metrics, offers, and product claims can change; none are treated as verified facts about Questra. Recommendations are design interpretations for Questra, not claims that any competitor feature should be replicated.
